## Why Tokenized Treasuries Hit $15B and Why That Number Matters

Tokenized US Treasuries are the cleanest version of this entire trend. You take a T-bill — backed by the full faith of the US government — wrap it in a blockchain token, and the yield flows to the token holder.

The $15 billion TVL milestone means institutional capital has validated the model. BlackRock, Franklin Templeton, Ondo, and Superstate are all running live products. The infrastructure is real. And the total US Treasury market is around $27 trillion — $15 billion on-chain is rounding error. We are very early.

What does yield actually look like? As of May 31, 2026 (APY fluctuates):

- **Franklin Templeton BENJI:** ~4.4% APY — $20 minimum, US retail access, T+1 liquidity
- **Ondo USDY:** ~4.5-5.0% APY — approximately $500 minimum, available to non-US retail
- **Superstate USCC:** ~4.2% APY — DeFi-native, composable across protocols
- **BlackRock BUIDL:** ~4.3% APY — $5M minimum, institutions only

For most people, BENJI is the obvious starting point. Small minimum, real T-bill backing, and you do not need to navigate DeFi protocols to use it. Non-US reader? USDY is the functional equivalent.

One thing nobody says upfront: these yields are rate-dependent. When the Federal Reserve eventually cuts, BENJI drops from 4.4% to maybe 3% within weeks as old bills mature and roll at lower rates. The people who build positions now are locking in near-peak rates. That window does not stay open indefinitely.

For a deeper breakdown of how these products stack up against DeFi alternatives, the [RWA yield on-chain comparison](/blog/rwa-yield-on-chain-buidl-vs-aave-2026) covers BUIDL, BENJI, Pendle, and Aave side by side.

**One-liner:** Tokenized Treasuries are boring, real, and working right now — treat them as the risk-free baseline for everything else on this list.

## Tokenized Gold: The 5,000-Year Hedge Goes On-Chain

Gold has been a store of value since around 700 BC. Its tokenized version has existed for about five years. The appeal: gold exposure without physically owning gold, plus in some cases a small yield on top.

The main options:

**PAX Gold (PAXG):** One token equals one fine troy ounce in a London vault (Paxos Trust Company). No yield by default — this is a gold token, not a yield product.

**Tether Gold (XAUT):** Same structure, Swiss custody. Also no base yield.

The yield question on gold tokens is genuinely complicated. Because physical gold generates no cash flow, any "yield" you earn comes entirely from lending demand — someone borrowing your gold tokens for short positions or hedging. That rate sits typically at 0.5-2% APY depending on market conditions (as of May 2026, APY fluctuates). Some protocols add token incentives on top, but those are inflationary yields layered over real demand, not organic income.

Confession: I tried to optimize my PAXG position in early 2026 and ended up in a three-protocol spaghetti situation. PAXG deposited on Protocol A, borrowed stablecoin against it at 2.1%, stablecoin deposited on Protocol B earning 3.2%. Net yield positive. Time managing it: not worth it. I untangled all of it in March 2026 and now keep a simple PAXG position on [Bybit](https://www.bybit.com/invite?ref=WKNA1V) for clean price exposure.

The real 2026 story for tokenized gold is not yield — it is collateral. More DeFi protocols now accept PAXG as collateral, which means you can deposit gold, borrow stablecoins against it at a conservative LTV, and deploy those stablecoins into yield products. If you are comfortable managing loan-to-value ratios, this unlocks meaningful capital efficiency. If that sentence made you nervous, just hold outright.

**One-liner:** Tokenized gold is a diversification and collateral play, not a yield product — going in expecting 8% APY will disappoint you.

## Tokenized Real Estate: The Interesting One Nobody Talks About

This is where the APY numbers get attention and the complexity goes up proportionally.

Tokenized real estate means you own a fraction of an actual property — an apartment building in Detroit, a commercial space in Austin, a vacation rental in Miami — via blockchain tokens. Rent income flows to token holders as yield.

The main platforms:

**RealT:** Tokenizes US residential rental properties. Listed properties report 8-12% APY from actual rental income (as of May 2026, APY fluctuates — vacancy affects each property individually). 183+ properties on the platform. Minimum approximately $50 per property token.

**Lofty:** Similar model, Algorand-based. Properties from $50 minimum, daily rental income distributions.

The honest breakdown of tokenized real estate — the things most articles skip:

Liquidity is low. These are not USDC. Exiting a position means selling to another buyer on the platform's secondary market. If few buyers are active when you want out, you could wait weeks. This is the biggest factor most first-time buyers underestimate.

Property-specific risk matters. One vacant unit in your specific property means no income from that token. You are not buying diversified real estate exposure — you are buying 123 Main Street, Detroit. Tenant turnover, local market conditions, and property management quality all affect your return directly.

Regulatory picture is less clear. Tokenized real estate navigates US securities law through LLC structures. The framework is less settled than T-bill products with institutional-grade legal teams behind them.

The upside nobody leads with: your token can appreciate if the underlying property appreciates. A tokenized Austin property from 2023 that has risen 15% in market value gives you both yield and price gain. That upside does not exist in T-bill products.

My personal calculus: tokenized real estate earns a 5-10% slice in a broader yield stack. Not a core holding for most people, but a genuinely different income source with a higher ceiling. The liquidity profile is the one thing to internalize before you commit.

## Risks You Should Not Skim

I have seen people treat RWA yield like a high-yield savings account with extra steps. It is not.

**Tokenized Treasuries:**
- Rate risk: Fed cuts compress yields within weeks — this is the structural limitation of the category
- Custodian risk: Franklin Templeton, Ondo, and Paxos all carry institutional counterparty exposure
- USDC depeg history (March 2023) is a reminder that dollar-pegged wrappers carry their own risks
- Regulatory: cleaner than the alternatives, but policy around tokenized securities continues to evolve

**Tokenized Gold:**
- Custodian risk: vault storage, third-party audit dependency
- No base yield without lending; lending adds protocol risk on top of price volatility
- Gold price moves independently of any yield you earn
- Some DeFi gold lending markets have thin liquidity, making yield unpredictable

**Tokenized Real Estate:**
- Vacancy in your specific property is your problem, not averaged away across a portfolio
- Low platform liquidity means potentially weeks to exit
- Regulatory ambiguity around securities classification under US law
- Platform operational risk: RealT and Lofty are companies with their own business continuity risks

**Cross-cutting:** The KelpDAO $292M exploit in April 2026 — which I broke down in the [DeFi yield safe alternatives post](/blog/defi-yield-safe-alternatives-after-kelpdao-hack-2026) — is a reminder that on-chain yield carries smart contract risk regardless of what the underlying asset is. Platform diversification is not optional; it is the job.

## FAQ

**What is the best RWA yield option for beginners in 2026?**

Franklin Templeton BENJI is the most accessible entry point — $20 minimum, US retail access, T+1 liquidity, backed by actual US Treasury bills. It pays approximately 4.4% APY (as of May 31, 2026, APY fluctuates). Non-US investors can use Ondo USDY as a functional equivalent with slightly higher yield potential.

**Can you earn passive income on tokenized gold?**

Tokenized gold tokens like PAXG (PAX Gold) and XAUT (Tether Gold) do not generate yield by default — they track gold prices. To earn yield, you lend your tokens on DeFi protocols where borrow demand exists, generating approximately 0.5-2.0% APY (as of May 2026, APY fluctuates). This adds smart contract risk on top of gold's existing price volatility.

**How does tokenized real estate yield compare to tokenized Treasuries?**

Tokenized real estate platforms like RealT report 8-12% APY from actual rental income (as of May 2026, APY fluctuates — vacancy affects individual properties directly). This is substantially higher than the 4-5% range for tokenized T-bill products. The premium reflects much lower liquidity: exiting tokenized real estate requires an OTC buyer, not T+1 or instant redemption.

**Will tokenized Treasury yields drop in 2026?**

Tokenized Treasury yields are directly tied to Federal Reserve policy. If the Fed cuts rates, products like BENJI and USDY compress within weeks as underlying T-bills mature and roll at the new lower rate. The current 4-5% range reflects an elevated rate environment. Private credit products like Pendle eACRED have longer lock-up periods and may maintain higher yields temporarily after any rate cuts.

**What is the minimum to start earning RWA yield on-chain?**

Franklin Templeton BENJI: $20 minimum. RealT tokenized real estate: approximately $50 per property token. Ondo USDY: approximately $500 minimum. Aave USDC (stablecoin lending as adjacent comparison): no stated minimum, though Ethereum mainnet gas costs make positions under $300-500 inefficient.

**Is tokenized real estate available to international investors?**

Availability varies by jurisdiction. RealT properties are US-based assets, and token purchase availability for non-US buyers depends on local securities law. Lofty has similar geographic considerations. Ondo USDY and Superstate USCC have broader international availability. Always verify local regulations before investing in any tokenized securities product.
