## The Slashing Risk Section (Don't Skip This)

My 30% restaking allocation could lose 3–5% in a slashing event. This isn't theoretical — EigenLayer's slashing mechanism activated in 2025, AVS rules are enforced, and operators who misbehave face consequences that propagate to delegators.

**What protects me in my current setup:**

ether.fi diversifies operator selection automatically across a curated set. I'm not delegating everything to a single operator running 15 AVSs simultaneously. This distributes but doesn't eliminate slashing risk.

My restaking position is completely separate from my DeFi borrowing collateral. The stETH I use on Aave is the 60% tranche — plain Lido staking, no restaking exposure. A slashing event wouldn't directly touch my Aave health factor.

**What doesn't protect me:**

A coordinated exploit of ether.fi's smart contracts. An operator error triggering simultaneous AVS slashing events. A protocol-level bug in EigenLayer itself. All audited. None guaranteed safe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What blended APY does the 60/30/10 Lido + EigenLayer strategy produce in May 2026?**

Approximately 3.8–4.5% blended APY, depending on ether.fi operator performance and AVS reward rates. The Lido portion contributes ~3–3.5% base staking yield; the ether.fi restaking portion contributes approximately 5–6.5% total yield (including base staking + restaking rewards). APY fluctuates — these are estimates, not guaranteed returns.

**Is it safe to have both Lido and EigenLayer exposure simultaneously?**

They're independent protocols, so a failure in one doesn't automatically affect the other. The main compounding factor is ETH price: both positions are denominated in ETH, so a significant price drop affects total dollar value across both. Slashing risk applies only to the restaking tranche, not the plain Lido staking tranche.

**Why use ether.fi instead of depositing directly into EigenLayer?**

ether.fi manages operator selection automatically and returns weETH — a liquid token usable in DeFi protocols. Direct EigenLayer deposits require choosing and monitoring operators yourself, and rewards must be claimed manually. ether.fi adds one smart contract layer but removes significant operational complexity. For large positions ($100K+ ETH), direct EigenLayer with manually selected established operators might be worth the overhead for the additional control.

**Should I wait for CLARITY Act resolution before allocating to DeFi yield?**

Waiting 12–18 months for regulatory resolution means leaving estimated yields on the table during that period. The post-CLARITY Act version of these protocols will likely have more capital in them (compressed APY) than today's. This is not a recommendation to act — assess your own risk tolerance and legal situation.

**How often do I need to check this position?**

Using ether.fi for the restaking tranche: monthly is sufficient. ether.fi handles operator delegation and auto-compounds reward returns into the weETH token price. The Aave position (wstETH collateral, WETH buffer) warrants a weekly glance at health factors if you're borrowing against it, less frequently if you're just earning supply yield.
