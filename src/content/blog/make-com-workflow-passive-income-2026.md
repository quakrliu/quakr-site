## What Is the Make.com Workflow Income Model?

You're not building an agency. You're not freelancing. You're building a product that a business rents from you every month.

The distinction matters. Freelancers trade time for money — $80/hour, repeat forever. Product builders build once and bill forever. Make.com workflows sit squarely in the second category.

A workflow is an automated sequence: when X happens in one app, Make.com does Y in another. New Shopify order → update Google Sheet → send Slack notification → create Airtable task. That sequence runs 24/7 without the client touching anything. You built it. They depend on it. They pay you to keep it running.

The average small business has 5–10 of these manual processes happening right now, costing them $50–$150/hour in staff time. You're not selling tech. You're selling back their Tuesday afternoons.

## How Do You Price It? (The Framework That Stopped Me Undercharging)

Three-part pricing formula:

**1. Base build fee** = hours to build × $150–$200/hour. A 4-hour build = $600–$800 minimum.

**2. Complexity multiplier** — Add 30–50% if the workflow touches 4+ apps, uses conditional logic, or handles financial data. My Perth client hit all three → $2,200.

**3. Monthly retainer** = 15–20% of build fee per month. $2,200 build → $330–$440/month. I charge her $350.

This retainer covers: monitoring the workflow runs (Make.com logs this automatically), fixing broken connections when apps update, adding minor steps as the client's process evolves.

The retainer is the whole game. It's what turns a one-time project into a passive income stream.

## How Do You Find Clients?

Honestly, this is the one part that isn't passive. You do have to find them. But you only need 5, and you only need to find them once.

My sourcing channels, ranked by effectiveness:

**LinkedIn DMs to small business owners** — Search "founder" or "operations manager" in your target niche. Message: "I noticed [specific manual process] is a common problem in [industry]. I automate that. Happy to show you a 10-minute demo." Reply rate: ~12%.

**Local Facebook business groups** — Post a specific use case. "If you're copy-pasting Shopify orders into spreadsheets every morning, I can automate that for $800 with a 30-day money-back guarantee." This is how I found the Perth client.

**Upwork for first clients** — Yes, it's competitive and the fees hurt. But your first 3 clients give you case studies. After that, you don't need Upwork.

**Referrals** — My Perth client referred two more e-commerce owners. These are your best clients: trust is already built, and they're in the same niche, so your workflow often needs minor tweaks rather than a full rebuild.

A quick note on tools: I use Make.com's Pro plan (~$16/month) to serve all my clients from one account using separate "organizations." You're not building on clients' accounts — you own the workflow. That's your negotiating position.

## The Risk Section (Read This Before You Start)

A few things that can go wrong:

**Client churn** — A client might stop needing the workflow (business closes, they hire someone to do it manually). Build for 5+ clients so one loss doesn't hurt badly. I keep a target of 6 active maintenance contracts minimum.

**API changes** — Make.com integrations break when apps update. In March 2026, Shopify changed their webhook format and three of my workflows needed fixing same day. Budget time for this — it's rare but real.

**Scope creep** — Clients will ask for "one small addition" that's actually 3 hours of work. Write a clear scope in your initial contract. Changes beyond the original spec are billed separately.

**Platform risk** — Make.com could change pricing or shut down (unlikely, they're well-funded). Keep workflow documentation so you can rebuild on n8n or Zapier if needed.

**Taxes** — Recurring income from automation services counts as self-employment income in most jurisdictions. If you're accepting crypto payments for any of this, [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/?fpr=4bsqmx) handles the tracking automatically.

This is what I do, not financial or legal advice. Your jurisdiction's rules may differ.

## FAQ

**How much does Make.com cost?**
Make.com Pro plan is approximately $16/month (as of May 2026; pricing fluctuates). This covers 10,000 operations/month — enough for 5–8 active client workflows depending on volume.

**Do I need to be a developer to build Make.com workflows?**
No. Make.com uses a visual drag-and-drop editor. Basic workflows take an hour to learn. Complex multi-branch logic takes a weekend to get comfortable with. No coding required.

**How long does it take to land the first client?**
My honest experience: 3–6 weeks from first outreach to signed contract. LinkedIn + local business groups are the fastest channels. Your first client will take longer; the referral machine starts after client 2–3.

**What if a client stops paying?**
You own the workflow. You can pause or delete it from your Make.com account. Build this into your contract: "Workflows are deactivated within 48 hours of missed payment." That gives you real control.

**How is this different from an automation agency?**
An agency builds custom projects for each client from scratch. The workflow income model focuses on repeatable templates in one or two niches — you build a workflow for an e-commerce brand once, then sell variations of it to 5 more e-commerce brands with minimal extra work. Higher margin, lower complexity.
